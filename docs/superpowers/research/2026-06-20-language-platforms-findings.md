## English learner dictionary entry-presentation patterns (Cambridge, Oxford OALD, Merriam-Webster Learner's / Britannica, Longman LDOCE, Collins COBUILD) — synthesized into a concrete data schema and detail-page recommendation for the Zhesen Vietnamese-targeted en/zh/es dictionary.

### Nền tảng
- **Cambridge Dictionary (Advanced Learner's)** (https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/issue)
  - Tính năng: Senses grouped by part of speech, each major sense headed by an ALL-CAPS GUIDE WORD in parentheses, e.g. 'issue noun [C] (SUBJECT)', '(PRODUCT)', '(SUPPLY)'. This is the single most useful navigation device for polysemous words.; Per-sense CEFR badge (B1, B2, C1, C2) rendered as a small pill immediately before the definition; idioms/phrases carry their own CEFR badge (e.g. 'at issue' = C2) plus register tags ('disapproving', 'formal').; Grammar/countability code shown inline next to POS as a bracketed link: '[C]', '[T]', '[C or U]'.; UK and US IPA shown SEPARATELY, each with its own speaker/audio button: uk /ˈɪʃ.uː/ button, us /ˈɪʃ.uː/ button (US-only single IPA in the American Dictionary tab).; Each sense leads with a bulleted list of typical collocations/patterns (e.g. 'environmental/ethical/personal issues', 'address an issue', 'side issue') BEFORE the example sentences, with the headword bolded in examples.; Collapsible 'More examples / Fewer examples' corpus block per sense; collapsible 'SMART Vocabulary: related words and phrases' and 'Thesaurus: synonyms, antonyms, and examples' boxes.; Idioms and Phrasal verbs are separate labeled sections ('Idiom', 'Phrasal verb') listed as cross-reference links to their own pages.; Multiple dictionary tabs for one headword (Learner's / American / Business English) with source attribution line per tab.
  - UX: Guide word in parentheses as the sense title — instantly scannable, language-independent label.; Per-sense (not just per-entry) CEFR badge so learners can filter by level.; Collocations surfaced first, examples second, under each sense.; Separate UK/US audio buttons sitting right next to each IPA string.; Progressive disclosure: examples and related-words boxes are collapsed by default.
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: Highest-value model for our detail page. The guide-word-in-parentheses pattern is the cleanest way to show ~2-3 top senses without a wall of text, and maps directly onto a new senses[].guideWord field. Per-sense CEFR badges replace our single entry-level 'level'. Separate UK/US IPA+audio is exactly what our pronunciations[] array (accent/ipa/audioUrl) already supports but the hero does not yet render.
- **Oxford Learner's Dictionaries (OALD)** (https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/issue_1)
  - Tính năng: Senses carry short lowercase 'shortcut' guide words ('topic of discussion', 'problem/worry', 'magazine/newspaper', 'making available/known', 'children') and a 'jump to other results' navigator.; Per-sense CEFR badge (B1, B2...) PLUS an Oxford 3000/5000 keyword marker (key icon) indicating high-frequency core vocabulary; header also carries OPAL (academic) Written/Spoken sublist tags.; BrE and NAmE IPA each with its own audio; 'Word Origin' (etymology) collapsible section.; Dedicated 'Oxford Collocations Dictionary' panel listing collocates grouped by type (adjective + noun, verb + noun, preposition).; Collapsible 'Extra Examples' block separate from the in-sense examples.; Idioms section and (for verbs) Phrasal verbs section kept distinct from numbered senses.; Definitions written within the Oxford 3000 controlled defining vocabulary so they stay learner-readable.
  - UX: Dual labeling: short guide word for navigation + CEFR badge + core-word marker on the same sense line.; Collocations grouped by grammatical pattern rather than a flat synonym list.; 'Extra examples' as opt-in expansion to keep the default view short.; Controlled defining vocabulary keeps glosses simple — relevant since our EN glosses target Vietnamese learners.
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: Confirms Cambridge's guide-word + per-sense-CEFR pattern and adds two adoptable ideas: a 'core vocabulary' marker (we could derive one from CEFR A1-B2 or a frequency rank), and collocations grouped by grammatical pattern (adj+n, v+n, prep) rather than an undifferentiated synonym chip cloud.
- **Merriam-Webster Learner's / Britannica Dictionary** (https://www.britannica.com/dictionary/issue)
  - Tính năng: Senses numbered 1,2,3,4 with letter sub-senses (1a, 1b); ordered by frequency/common-sense-first, not historical order.; Grammar labels are explicit and bracketed: nouns get '[count]' / '[noncount]' / '[singular]' / '[plural]'; verbs get '[+ object]' / '[no object]'. These appear right where the sense begins.; COBUILD-influenced, full and plain-English definitions; examples are complete sentences in a bulleted list with the headword italicized, and a '[+] more examples' expander per sense.; Idioms/phrases each get their OWN bold heading (e.g. 'at issue', 'force the issue', 'have issues', 'take issue with', 'without issue') with register/domain tags ('informal', 'law', 'formal') and their own numbered sub-senses + examples.; Derived words appear as run-on entries at the end with their own POS and inflection, e.g. '— issuer  noun, plural issuers [count]'.; Headword line shows inflections: 'plural issues' for nouns; 'issues; issued; issuing' for verbs. Single US IPA /ˈɪˌʃuː/ with one audio button.; 'N ENTRIES FOUND' homograph navigator listing issue (noun), issue (verb), standard-issue (adjective), etc.
  - UX: Frequency-first sense ordering with most-common meaning as sense 1 — explicitly stated learner-dictionary policy.; Grammar labels ([count]/[noncount], [+object]/[no object]) inline at the start of each sense.; Inflections/word-forms shown on the headword line.; Idioms as first-class headed blocks with their own register tag and examples, not just cross-reference links.; Derived terms as run-on entries carrying their own POS + inflection.
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: Strongest model for the 'word-family / derived' requirement: derived terms carry their own POS and inflected forms, not just a bare string — so our flat relations[] (string only) should become structured derived items with {text, pos, gloss}. Also the cleanest example-presentation pattern to copy: examples attached to the specific sense, headword highlighted, '+ more examples' expander. Sense-ordering policy (most common first) should drive our senseOrder.
- **Longman Dictionary of Contemporary English (LDOCE)** (https://www.ldoceonline.com/dictionary/issue)
  - Tính năng: SIGNPOSTS: each sense is prefixed by a bold capitalized guide word (SUBJECT, PROVIDE, AVAILABLE, etc.) — same concept as Cambridge guide words, used to jump to the right sense in long entries.; Frequency markers: three circles ●●● / ●●○ and S1/S2/S3 (spoken) + W1/W2/W3 (written) bands marking the 3000/9000 most important words; defined using a ~2000-word controlled defining vocabulary.; Most common meaning shown first; senses numbered within each POS.; BrE/AmE IPA with a '$' separating variants (/ˈɪʃuː, ˈɪsjuː $ ˈɪʃuː/); 88,000 example sentences with British or American audio, examples grouped under an 'Examples from the Corpus' block.; Dedicated COLLOCATION boxes (verb/adjective collocates, common patterns) and integrated thesaurus boxes; 2000+ collocation boxes online.; Related multi-word entries surfaced under 'More results' (e.g. issue forth).
  - UX: Signpost guide words for sense navigation (parallels Cambridge).; Frequency bands (spoken vs written, three-dot importance) as a compact visual cue of how worth-learning a word is.; Controlled defining vocabulary for readable glosses.; Examples bundled in a labeled corpus block with native audio.; Collocation and thesaurus as dedicated boxes.
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: Reinforces signpost/guide-word as a near-universal pattern (4 of 5 dictionaries use it). The spoken/written frequency band is a differentiator we could approximate from corpus rank if we have it. Confirms collocations deserve a dedicated section distinct from synonyms — matching our requirement to keep synonyms / derived / compounds separate.
- **Collins (COBUILD Advanced Learner's + Collins English Dictionary)** (https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/issue)
  - Tính năng: Word-frequency band at the top: a visual meter plus tooltip 'Extremely Common. issue is one of the 1000 most commonly used words in the Collins dictionary'.; Word forms shown inline right after the headword: 'Word forms: issues, issuing, issued', each with an audio control.; Per-sense POS+grammar label as running text ('countable noun', 'singular noun', 'uncountable noun', 'verb') with a per-sense CEFR badge (B1+, B2) linking to a CEFR-labels explainer.; COBUILD full-sentence definitions ('An issue is an important subject that people are arguing about or discussing.') — definition embeds the headword in natural context.; Grammar pattern boxes attached to examples as bracketed links: '[+ of]', '[VERB noun]', '[be VERB-ed + with]', '[VERB + from]', linking to a grammar-pattern reference.; Per-sense inline 'Synonyms:' list (topic, point, matter, problem...) plus a 'More Synonyms of issue' link to the thesaurus; every example has auto-generated TTS audio.; Separate BrE/AmE IPA blocks ('British English pronunciation' / 'American English pronunciation') and a fuller, more traditional 'issue in British English' Collins English Dictionary entry below the COBUILD one.; 'See also side issue' cross-references inline as their own numbered slot.
  - UX: Frequency meter with plain-language tooltip — friendly way to convey how core a word is.; Word forms/inflections inline on the headword line, each individually pronounceable.; COBUILD full-sentence definitions (good for comprehension; heavier than gloss-style).; Grammar-pattern tags on examples linking to a pattern reference.; Inline per-sense synonyms with a 'more' link to a full thesaurus.
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: Best model for word-forms/inflections presentation (inline on headword line, individually audio-enabled) and for per-sense inline synonyms with a 'more' link. The frequency meter + plain tooltip is a low-cost, learner-friendly cue. Grammar-pattern tags suggest our examples could optionally carry a pattern label. The two-tier entry (learner COBUILD on top, fuller traditional below) mirrors our possible 'top 2-3 senses then expand' approach.

### Khuyến nghị áp dụng
- [M, cần data] **Add a per-sense guide word + per-sense CEFR/level + register label to DictSense, and render the top 2-3 senses as 'POS [grammar] (GUIDEWORD) — gloss' blocks** — Guide words in parentheses/signposts are used by 4 of 5 major learner dictionaries (Cambridge, Oxford, Longman, and effectively Britannica's short sense tags) and are the single most effective device for letting a learner scan a polysemous word and pick the right meaning fast. Per-sense CEFR (Cambridge, Oxford, Collins all do it) lets us show the requested 2-3 TOP senses by ranking on level+frequency. Our current DictSense only has {pos, glossVi, glossEn, senseOrder} — it cannot express a guide word, a level, or a register tag, so the page currently renders an undifferentiated numbered list. Concrete schema change: DictSense += guideWord?: string | null; level?: string | null (CEFR per sense); register?: string | null; grammarCode?: string | null (e.g. 'C','U','C or U','+obj','no obj'). Move the entry-level `level` rendering off the hero onto each sense.
- [M, cần data] **Attach examples (and collocations) to each sense instead of one flat global examples[] array, with a collapsible 'more examples' expander** — Every dictionary studied attaches examples to the specific sense they illustrate (Cambridge bulleted under each guide-word block, Britannica/Longman/Collins under each numbered sense), and all use progressive disclosure ('More examples / Fewer examples', '[+] more examples'). Our ExampleList currently renders detail.examples as one global list divorced from senses, which is the biggest structural divergence from real learner dictionaries. Concrete change: move DictExample[] inside DictSense (senses[].examples) with senseOrder linkage, keep a small N visible and collapse the rest. Also add a senses[].collocations field (Cambridge/Oxford/Longman surface collocations per sense, grouped by pattern adj+n / v+n / prep).
- [S] **Render UK + US IPA each with its own audio button in the hero, sourced from the existing pronunciations[] array** — Cambridge, Oxford, Longman and Collins all present BrE and NAmE IPA as distinct, individually-audioed items; this is a core expectation of a learner dictionary. The data model already supports it (DictEntryDetail.pronunciations: {accent, ipa, audioUrl}[]), but LookupHero only renders the single scalar detail.ipa and one headword AudioButton. This is a pure rendering fix with no schema change: map over pronunciations[] and show 'uk /…/ [audio]  us /…/ [audio]'. Low effort, high perceived-quality gain.
- [M, cần data] **Split the single relations[] cloud into three distinct, structured sections — Synonyms/Antonyms, Derived (word family), and Compounds/Phrases — with POS on derived items** — The task explicitly requires separate synonyms / derived / compounds sections plus a word-family section. Britannica shows derived terms as run-on entries carrying their OWN part of speech and inflections ('— issuer noun, plural issuers'); Cambridge/Oxford keep idioms, phrasal verbs, and collocations in distinct blocks. Our RelatedWords currently flattens everything to {relationType, relatedText:string} chips, losing POS and the word-family grouping. Concrete change: keep synonyms/antonyms as chips, but model derived terms as {text, pos, gloss?} so the word-family section can show base+POS, and give compounds/phrasal-verbs/idioms their own headed lists (matching how all five dictionaries separate these).
- [S, cần data] **Add a lightweight frequency/core-word indicator and order senses most-common-first** — Collins shows a frequency meter ('one of the 1000 most commonly used words'), Longman shows ●●● S1/W1 bands, Oxford marks Oxford 3000/5000 core words, and Merriam-Webster Learner's explicitly orders senses by commonness rather than history. For Vietnamese learners deciding what is worth learning, a single 'core word' badge or frequency tier is a cheap, high-signal cue, and most-common-first sense ordering matches every learner dictionary's policy. Implementation can reuse senseOrder for ordering; the badge can be derived from CEFR (A1-B2 = core) or a corpus rank if available in the lex pipeline.

## Bilingual/contextual dictionaries and example-sentence sourcing — research to fix our corrupted, mis-spaced, mistranslated example data and improve dictionary UX

### Nền tảng
- **Tatoeba** (https://tatoeba.org)
  - Tính năng: Community-curated sentence database, 400+ languages, every sentence has a stable integer id and ISO 639-3 language code; translations are modeled as LINKS between sentence ids (not strings), so one sentence maps to many translations reciprocally.; Bulk downloads refreshed every Saturday with documented column formats: sentences.csv (id, lang, text), sentences_detailed.csv (id, lang, text, USERNAME, date_added, date_modified), links.csv (sentence_id, translation_id), tags.csv (sentence_id, tag), sentences_with_audio.csv (sentence_id, audio_id, username, LICENSE, attribution_url), and a separate sentences_CC0 export.; Per-sentence provenance and trust signals: owner username, contributor self-reported language skill, sentence OK rating/tag, semantic tags ('proverb','vulgar','archaic'), and CK's 900k+ hand-proofread English list.; Per-recording audio with explicit per-file license (CC BY / CC BY-SA / CC BY-NC / none) and attribution URL — license travels WITH each row, not assumed globally.; Dual licensing: bulk text CC BY 2.0 FR (attribution), plus a CC0 subset exported separately for attribution-free reuse.
  - UX: Sentence-centric, not headword-centric: you look at a sentence and fan out to all its translations in any language.; Each sentence card shows owner, audio play button, and links to its translations and tags.
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: This is our cleanest existing source — I confirmed our Tatoeba-sourced rows in lex.examples are well-spaced with real sentence-level Vietnamese translations (e.g. 'The performance of electric cars has improved.' / 'Hiệu suất của ô tô điện đang được cải thiện.'), in direct contrast to our corrupted Cambridge rows. We should (1) re-pull from the official weekly dumps using sentences_detailed + links to get clean text plus owner provenance we are currently discarding, (2) populate lex.examples.audio_url/audio_source from sentences_with_audio with the correct per-row license into our existing tier column, (3) store the source sentence id in provenance jsonb so examples are traceable and de-dupable, and (4) prefer CK-proofread / OK-tagged sentences and drop 'vulgar' tags (our zh set currently contains an offensive sentence). Tatoeba EN<->VI link coverage is thin, so pair it with OPUS for VI volume (see top recommendations).
- **Reverso Context** (https://context.reverso.net)
  - Tính năng: Examples mined from millions of real bilingual texts (movie subtitles, official documents, product descriptions, news), aligned source<->human-translation pairs rather than dictionary-authored sentences.; Big-data + ML ranking selects and orders examples; users vote against bad examples to continuously retrain ranking.; One entry surfaces multiple translations of a word, each with its own example set, plus frequency, conjugation, synonyms, and definitions in one click.; Transliteration of example sentences into Latin alphabet when listening (directly relevant to our missing zh pinyin reading).
  - UX: Click a translation chip to FILTER the example list down to only sentences using that specific sense/translation — turns a long flat example list into a sense-scoped one.; The searched word and its translation are bolded/highlighted inside each example sentence on both sides.; Per-example 'report/vote against' control feeding quality back into ranking.; Full-sentence natural audio playback, not just headword audio.; Translations ordered by real-world frequency so the most common rendering is first.
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: The single highest-value UX pattern to copy: bind examples to a specific SENSE and let the user filter examples by sense/translation. Our schema already supports this — lex.examples.sense_id exists but we attach examples at entry level (getEntryDetail joins examples!examples_entry_id_fkey and ExampleList renders one flat list). Wiring examples to senses + a sense filter chip would immediately make our examples feel curated. Also adopt: bold the headword inside each example, full-sentence audio (we already have AudioButton on examples), and a lightweight per-example downvote to flag corrupted entries like our Cambridge rows.
- **Linguee** (https://www.linguee.com)
  - Tính năng: Web-crawled bilingual texts auto-split into parallel sentence pairs.; Each candidate pair scored by a human-trained ML quality estimator; users can also rate translations manually, continuously retraining the scorer (hybrid automated + human QC).; Distinguishes high-confidence professionally-translated sources (e.g. EU/UN, corporate sites) from lower-confidence crawl, and shows the SOURCE WEBSITE for every example so the reader can judge trust.; Separates a curated editorial dictionary section (vetted translations) from the 'external sources' example section (crawled, attributed, quality-scored).
  - UX: Every example links to and names its source page — provenance is visible inline, letting the user assess trustworthiness themselves.; Two-tier layout: trusted dictionary results on top, attributed real-usage examples below, visually separated so users know which is authoritative.; Quality/confidence affects ordering; low-confidence pairs are pushed down or flagged.
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: Two adoptable ideas. First, the two-tier trust model maps onto our schema's open vs personal tier and source_id: render curated/open-licensed examples prominently and visibly attribute every example to its source (we store source_id but ExampleList shows nothing about provenance). Second, Linguee's source-confidence scoring is the conceptual blueprint for a cleanup pass on our 11k examples — compute a per-example quality score (spacing sanity, translation-vs-sentence length ratio, language-detect) and hide/demote failures rather than deleting blindly. This directly addresses our Cambridge corruption where text is mangled AND translation_vi is just the headword gloss.
- **WordReference** (https://www.wordreference.com)
  - Tính năng: Entries organized by sense: 'Principal Translations' first, then 'Additional Translations', each sense with its own short example phrase showing the word in context.; Dedicated Collocations section (e.g. wordreference.com/EnglishCollocations/<word>) listing the word's common partner words/phrases, grouped by grammatical role.; Compound forms and idioms listed as their own sub-entries under the headword.; Inline clickable words for instant secondary lookup, audio pronunciation, full conjugation tables.; Moderated human discussion forums attached per word for nuance/edge cases not capturable in a static entry.
  - UX: Sense-grouped table layout where each row = one sense + its POS + a contextual example, so the example is never orphaned from the meaning it illustrates.; Collocations presented as their own browsable section, distinct from free example sentences.; Tap-any-word-in-an-example to look it up (we already have TappableText doing exactly this in ExampleList).
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: Reinforces the Reverso lesson with a cleaner information architecture: bind one example to one sense and lay senses out as the spine of the entry. Our SenseList and ExampleList are currently separate sections; merging them (example shown under the sense it belongs to) matches WordReference and is low effort given lex.senses + lex.examples.sense_id already exist. Their separate Collocations section also validates surfacing lex.lex_relations of type 'collocation' as a distinct block rather than mixing into generic 'related words' — our RelatedWords component already takes relation_type and could split collocations out.
- **Glosbe** (https://glosbe.com)
  - Tính năng: Translation-memory model: example sentences come from parallel corpora and crowd contributions across a very long tail of languages (incl. rare ones).; REST API for dictionary translations + translation-memory example pairs.; Each example pair is shown with attribution to its corpus/source.
  - UX: Word entry shows translations on top, then a scrollable list of real translation-memory example pairs underneath.; Examples attributed to their originating corpus.
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: Mostly a cautionary case: Glosbe is openly described as crowdsourced with variable, sometimes unreliable quality and noisy web-sourced examples — the exact failure mode we already have. Lesson: do NOT ingest unfiltered crowd/web examples without a quality gate and source attribution. Its only positive for us is coverage of rare pairs and the principle of always attributing each example to its corpus, which our source_id column is built for but our UI does not yet surface.
- **Wiktionary (via Kaikki / wiktextract)** (https://kaikki.org)
  - Tính năng: wiktextract fully expands Wiktionary templates/Lua and emits machine-readable JSON per entry: senses, usage examples, quotations (with cited author/work/date), translations, pronunciation+audio URLs, inflections, etymology, and lexical/semantic relations.; Distinguishes two example types: hand-written illustrative 'usage examples' attached per sense, and sourced 'quotations/citations' with full bibliographic attribution.; Editorial guideline favors a few good, sense-tailored illustrative quotations directly in the entry; large quote dumps go to a separate Citations namespace.; Already our open EN/ES source (wiktionary-en/-es sources in our lex.sources).
  - UX: Examples nested under the specific sense (definition) they illustrate, with the headword bolded in the example.; Citations carry author/work/year so the example is verifiable and dated.
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: Kaikki gives sense-attached examples for free in clean JSON, which fixes both our problems at once: examples arrive already bound to a sense_id (fills the sense linkage Reverso/WordReference call for) and arrive correctly spaced (no HTML-scrape corruption like our Cambridge rows). For EN/ES we should pull examples from the kaikki dump's per-sense 'examples' field instead of scraping rendered HTML. It will not give Vietnamese translations though — those need MT (flag gloss_vi_is_mt, which our schema already has) or pairing with Tatoeba/OPUS VI links.

## Pronunciation & Phonetics UX + IPA Typography for dictionary/language-learning web apps

### Nền tảng
- **Forvo** (https://forvo.com/)
  - Tính năng: Crowdsourced human-recorded audio (NO TTS) in 400+ languages, recorded by verified native speakers; Multiple recordings per word so learners hear regional accent variation side by side (e.g. a Spanish word returns Spain / Mexico / Argentina / Colombia speakers); Per-recording metadata: speaker country/region, male/female voice; users pick which accent to hear; Community quality controls + demand-driven expansion (words get recorded when learners request them); Paid Pronunciation API/data licensing for embedding the audio into third-party apps
  - UX: List multiple audio rows per headword, each tagged with a flag/region label and speaker gender; Treat accent as a first-class attribute of each recording, not a single 'the' pronunciation; Let users compare accents in-place rather than navigating away
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: Directly validates our existing DictPron[] {accent, ipa, audioUrl} model: render ONE row per accent with a region label, not just detail.audioUrl. Forvo is also the realistic source/fallback for human zh/es/en audio where we lack our own recordings. Crowd model is overkill for us, but the 'one labelled audio row per accent' layout is the pattern to copy.
- **YouGlish** (https://youglish.com/)
  - Tính năng: Pronunciation-in-context: searches 100M+ YouTube tracks and plays real clips where the word is actually spoken; Accent filter (US / UK / AUS and others) before playback; Shows the word inside a real sentence with synced captions, so learners get prosody and natural usage, not isolated citation form; Embeddable Widget + free JS API; paid REST API tiers (Hobby ~€25/mo 500 req/day, Small Business ~€300/mo 10k req/day, Corporate custom)
  - UX: Accent selector as a top-level control that re-filters the result set; 'Next clip' navigation to hear many speakers say the same word; Caption highlighting of the target word during playback
  - Liên hệ với Zhesen: A drop-in enrichment for our example sentences: the YouGlish embeddable widget could power a 'hear it in real videos' affordance on the lookup page without us hosting any audio.
